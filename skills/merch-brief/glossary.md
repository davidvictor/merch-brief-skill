# merch-brief — Materials & Technique Glossary

A controlled vocabulary for writing `BRIEF.md` and building generation prompts. Use the right term in the right place and the brief reads like a real tech pack; the prompts produce manufacturer-grade outputs instead of fashion-blog vagueness.

Each entry: **term** — definition · *use when: contextual guidance*. Each section ends with a "reference points" line — 2–3 brands or mills that exemplify that register, for taste calibration only.

---

## 1 · Fabric weights

Two scales: **GSM** (grams per square meter, metric) and **oz/yd²** (ounces per square yard, US). Convert: `1 oz/yd² ≈ 33.91 GSM`.

- **Lightweight** — under ~150 GSM (under ~4.5 oz). · *use when: shirting, base layers, summer tees, linings.*
- **Mid-weight** — 150–270 GSM (4.5–8 oz). · *use when: standard tees, polos, light hoodies, button-downs.*
- **Heavyweight** — 270–400 GSM (8–12 oz). · *use when: premium tees, work shirts, mid-weight outerwear, structured hoodies — the default for "elevated merch."*
- **Super-heavyweight** — 400+ GSM (12 oz+). · *use when: raw denim, work jackets, drop-shoulder loopback fleece, "boxy" heavyweight tees.*
- **Loopback** — fleece woven with looped reverse face (unbrushed). · *use when: premium sweats; opposite of "brushed-back" which is fuzzier.*

*Reference points: Champion Reverse Weave, Lady White Co., Loopwheeler.*

## 2 · Weaves & knit structures

- **Jersey** — single-knit, smooth face, textured reverse. · *use when: tees, soft pullovers; default knit.*
- **Interlock** — double-knit jersey, both faces smooth, denser hand. · *use when: premium tees that should feel substantial without being heavy.*
- **Rib knit** — knit with vertical ribs; stretches across grain. · *use when: cuffs, hems, collars, fitted ribbed bodies.*
- **French terry** — knit with looped reverse face. · *use when: lighter sweats and pullovers; the polo-knit cousin of fleece.*
- **Brushed-back fleece** — French terry brushed on the reverse for warmth. · *use when: heavyweight hoodies and crewnecks; signals "premium sweatshirt."*
- **Piqué** — small textural weave with raised cells. · *use when: polos, rugbies; signals "country club / paddock register."*
- **Plain weave (taffeta)** — over-one, under-one. · *use when: shirts, linings, light nylons.*
- **Poplin** — fine plain weave with a slight crosswise rib. · *use when: dress shirts, summer trousers; reads "refined."*
- **Oxford** — basket weave (multiple wefts × multiple warps), often two-tone. · *use when: button-downs, casual shirting; reads "heritage prep."*
- **Twill** — diagonal-rib weave (chinos, denim, gabardine). · *use when: chinos, work shirts, jackets; signals "structured + durable."*
- **Herringbone** — broken twill with V-shaped pattern. · *use when: heritage tailoring, premium work jackets, sport coats.*
- **Chambray** — plain weave, colored warp + white weft (denim's lighter cousin). · *use when: summer work shirts, "softer than denim" register.*
- **Denim** — heavy twill, indigo-dyed warp + white weft. · *use when: jeans, jackets, overshirts; everything from raw to washed.*
- **Corduroy** — cut-pile fabric with vertical ridges ("wales"). · *use when: heritage trousers, overshirts; wale count (e.g. "8-wale," "16-wale") specifies coarseness.*
- **Satin weave** — long floats giving a glossy face. · *use when: linings, lapel facings, bomber jacket bodies.*
- **Supplex / paneled nylon** — soft, drapey nylon weave with a matte hand. · *use when: paddock jackets, track tops, premium windbreakers.*
- **Ripstop** — plain weave with reinforcing grid. · *use when: outdoor / techwear, lightweight outerwear.*
- **Bouclé** — looped novelty yarn weave. · *use when: knit cardigans, women's outerwear; signals "textural luxury."*

*Reference points: Permanent Style for shirting weaves, Reigning Champ for terry/fleece, RRL / Engineered Garments for denim and twill, Auralee for refined plain weaves.*

## 3 · Fits & silhouettes

- **Boxy** — wide-cut, short body, dropped shoulder, square hem. · *use when: heavyweight tees, modern hoodies, "skate" register.*
- **Cropped** — body shortened relative to standard length. · *use when: jackets, hoodies, knits sitting at high-waist or above.*
- **Oversized** — relaxed everywhere, generous shoulder and torso. · *use when: streetwear, modern paddock jackets.*
- **Relaxed** — eased fit without being oversized. · *use when: chinos, button-downs; the ALD/Aimé default.*
- **Slim** — close to the body without being skinny. · *use when: tailored pieces, contemporary suiting.*
- **Tapered** — narrowing toward the hem. · *use when: trousers, sweats, joggers.*
- **Straight** — consistent leg width from knee to hem. · *use when: jeans, chinos, work trousers; the heritage default.*
- **A-line** — flaring out toward the hem. · *use when: skirts, dresses, swing coats.*
- **Dropped shoulder** — shoulder seam falls below the natural shoulder. · *use when: oversized/boxy tees and hoodies; signals "modern" not "classic."*
- **Raglan sleeve** — sleeve seam runs diagonally from underarm to neckline. · *use when: baseball tees, athletic pieces, premium sweats.*
- **Set-in sleeve** — sleeve seam at the natural shoulder (classic). · *use when: tailored shirts, button-ups, traditional outerwear.*
- **Kimono sleeve** — body and sleeve cut from one piece, no shoulder seam. · *use when: drapey knits, robes, soft outerwear.*
- **Gusseted** — extra panel inserted for movement at high-stress points. · *use when: workwear crotches, underarms, behind-the-knee construction.*
- **Bias-cut** — fabric cut at 45° to the grain, for drape and stretch. · *use when: skirts, dresses, soft blouses.*
- **Tailored** — fitted through the body with internal structure (canvas, pad). · *use when: blazers, sport coats, structured trousers.*

*Reference points: Drake's for relaxed tailoring, Aimé Leon Dore for relaxed-but-precise menswear, Lady White Co. for boxy heavyweight tees.*

## 4 · Seams & construction

- **Flatlock seam** — panels joined edge-to-edge with no overlap; flattest possible seam. · *use when: knits, activewear, swimwear, anything that needs to feel "premium and seamless."*
- **Coverstitch** — twin-needle finish on the face with a looped underside. · *use when: hems on tees, sweats; reads as "modern athletic."*
- **Topstitching** — visible second row of stitching at a seam or hem. · *use when: structured pieces (jeans, chinos, work jackets); signals construction quality.*
- **Single-needle stitching** — one needle, one row; finer, slower, premium. · *use when: dress shirts; "single-needle tailoring" is a flex.*
- **Twin-needle stitching** — two parallel rows. · *use when: tee hems, work-shirt details; cheaper than single-needle but more durable than coverstitch.*
- **Bartack** — short, dense reinforcement stitch at stress points. · *use when: belt loops, pocket corners, fly bottoms; specify in any tech pack.*
- **French seam** — seam enclosed within itself, no raw edges. · *use when: refined shirting, linings, women's pieces.*
- **Blind hem** — nearly invisible hem stitched from the inside. · *use when: tailored trousers, dresses.*
- **Piping** — fabric-covered cord sewn into a seam for a raised contrasting edge. · *use when: paddock jackets, varsity, retro athletic; the "contrast piping" signature.*
- **Binding** — strip of fabric wrapping a raw edge. · *use when: necklines, armholes on knits; tonal or contrast.*
- **Facing** — interior fabric layer supporting a seam or edge. · *use when: lapels, collars, plackets.*
- **Placket** — fabric band enclosing a slit (button-front, polo, rugby). · *use when: any partial or full button opening.*
- **Yoke** — fitted panel at the shoulders or hips. · *use when: western shirts, work shirts, ranch / heritage register.*
- **Selvedge edge** — self-finished woven edge, often colored. · *use when: heritage denim, premium shirting; the visible selvedge ID is a flex.*

*Reference points: Iron Heart (visible heavy stitching), Engineered Garments (mixed seams), Drake's (single-needle shirting).*

## 5 · Embellishment — embroidery

- **Satin stitch** — closely spaced parallel stitches with a smooth, glossy face. · *use when: bold logos, lettering, fills; the embroidery default.*
- **Chain stitch** — looped, rope-like line of stitches. · *use when: retro scripts, outlines, decorative borders; signals "vintage / heritage."*
- **Lockstitch / fill stitch** — dense flat fill, machine-standard. · *use when: large filled shapes; pair with satin-stitched outline.*
- **Chenille** — looped pile creating a fuzzy, plush surface. · *use when: varsity letterforms, retro patches; signals "collegiate / archive."*
- **3D puff** — embroidery over a foam underlay, raised off the fabric. · *use when: hat-front logos, bold streetwear hits; signals "modern caps / hype."*
- **Appliqué** — fabric shape attached to a base and stitched around the edge. · *use when: large logos on knits, varsity letters, patchwork pieces.*
- **Patchwork** — multiple appliqué pieces composing an image or texture. · *use when: heritage-feel jackets, "made on the road" tour merch register.*
- **Merrowed edge** — overlocked decorative edge around a patch. · *use when: classic woven/embroidered patches; signals "vintage uniform."*
- **Tonal embroidery** — thread color matches the fabric for low-contrast detail. · *use when: subtle hits on premium knits, jackets — restraint signal.*
- **Direct embroidery vs. patch** — direct = stitched into the garment; patch = sewn on. · *use when: specifying production method; patches are more flexible, direct embroidery is cleaner.*

*Reference points: Bode (chain-stitched hand-embroidery), Aimé Leon Dore (direct embroidery and chenille), Reigning Champ (tonal-on-tonal embroidery).*

## 6 · Embellishment — print methods

- **Plastisol** — opaque PVC-based ink sitting on top of the fabric. · *use when: bright graphics on any color, durability matters; the industry default.*
- **Water-based** — ink that soaks into the fabric; soft hand. · *use when: light-color garments, "you can't feel the print"; modern premium tees.*
- **Discharge print** — bleach-based process that *removes* dye and re-colors. · *use when: dark 100% cotton garments where the print should feel like part of the fabric; signals "vintage tee."*
- **Puff print** — plastisol additive that swells under heat. · *use when: 3D logo hits on hoodies, retro graphics, streetwear; signals "playful / 90s."*
- **High-density print** — thick plastisol cured into a raised flat block. · *use when: modern streetwear graphics with a "tactile" surface.*
- **Foil print** — adhesive printed, then metallic foil pressed on top. · *use when: holiday capsules, anniversary pieces, premium accents.*
- **Flock print** — short fiber pile bonded to fabric for velvet feel. · *use when: varsity numbers, retro lettering; signals "athletic heritage."*
- **Sublimation** — dye-transferred into polyester via heat; print becomes part of the fabric. · *use when: all-over prints, jersey graphics, charts/maps on synthetic; only works on polyester.*
- **DTG (Direct-to-Garment)** — inkjet directly onto the fabric. · *use when: short runs, photographic detail; not as durable as plastisol.*
- **DTF (Direct-to-Film)** — print on film, heat-transfer onto garment. · *use when: small detailed graphics, mixed-fabric runs; modern alternative to vinyl heat transfer.*
- **Heat transfer / vinyl** — cut vinyl applied via heat. · *use when: numbers, names, athletic decoration on jerseys.*
- **Photochromic / glow / metallic shimmer** — specialty plastisol additives. · *use when: novelty pieces, capsule storytelling; use sparingly.*

*Reference points: Online Ceramics (discharge + water-based), Brain Dead (plastisol + puff + flock combinations), Stüssy (multi-color plastisol).*

## 7 · Washes & finishes

- **Garment-dyed** — finished garments dyed after construction; uneven, soft hand. · *use when: solid-color tees, sweats, hoodies; the "premium vintage" signal.*
- **Pigment-dyed** — pigments sitting on the fabric surface (not absorbed); fades into a vintage look. · *use when: soft, intentionally-uneven solid colors; signals "lived in from day one."*
- **Overdye** — dyeing a pre-finished/pre-printed garment a second color. · *use when: complex tonal effects, capsule "tinted" releases.*
- **Acid wash** — chlorine + pumice creating mottled, snowflake fading. · *use when: 80s/90s retro denim revival, novelty pieces.*
- **Stone wash** — pumice-tumbled for even fade and softness. · *use when: standard denim, work jackets, anywhere the garment should look "broken in."*
- **Enzyme wash** — cellulase enzymes breaking down surface fibers. · *use when: knits and denim that should feel soft and faded without harsh chemicals; eco-coded.*
- **Mineral wash** — tumbled with mineral abrasives for vintage distress. · *use when: heavyweight tees that should feel decade-old.*
- **Sun-faded / surf wash** — fading mimicking natural sun exposure on the shoulders/sleeves. · *use when: surf and beach-coded brands, "made by the sun" feel.*
- **Distressed** — intentional wear: holes, frays, abrasion. · *use when: rock-and-roll capsules, vintage-reference drops; specify locations precisely in tech pack.*
- **Raw / unwashed** — finished but not washed; will shrink and bleed. · *use when: heritage denim drops, "break it in yourself" register.*
- **Sanforized** — pre-shrunk under steam + tension; minimal post-wash shrinkage. · *use when: any non-raw cotton garment; the manufacturing default.*
- **Mercerized** — cotton treated with caustic soda for sheen and dye uptake. · *use when: polos, premium cotton knits that should have a slight gloss.*

*Reference points: Stan Ray (garment-dyed solids), Real McCoy's (raw denim & period-correct washes), Buck Mason (mineral-washed heavyweight tees).*

## 8 · Trims & hardware

### Labels
- **Satin woven label** — smooth, slightly glossy woven label. · *use when: inside-neck branding for premium pieces; soft against skin.*
- **Damask woven label** — denser, higher-contrast weave. · *use when: vintage uniform / archive register; sleeve, hem, or chest labels.*
- **Jacquard label** — most detailed woven label (multi-color patterns). · *use when: highly graphic brand marks, intricate logos.*
- **Taffeta label** — crisp, lower-cost woven. · *use when: care labels, hem flags.*
- **Grosgrain label** — ribbed-edge woven, often used as decorative hem tape. · *use when: heritage interior trim, hem labels.*
- **Printed satin tag** — printed (not woven) inside-neck label. · *use when: budget tier; reads less premium than woven.*

### Patches
- **Jacron patch** — coated-paper "leather-look" patch, durable + vegan. · *use when: jeans back-yoke patches, work jackets; the manufacturing standard for denim back-patches.*
- **Real leather patch** — natural leather, deboss/emboss or laser engraved. · *use when: high-end denim, heritage outerwear; premium signal.*
- **Rubber patch** — silicone or PVC raised patch. · *use when: outerwear, techwear, athletic; modern register.*
- **Woven patch** — woven-fabric patch with merrowed edge. · *use when: uniform / paddock / heritage register.*

### Closures
- **Horn button** — natural buffalo/cattle horn. · *use when: blazers, premium shirts; reads "tailored heritage."*
- **Corozo button** — natural ivory-nut button (vegetable ivory). · *use when: dress shirts, knitwear; subtle premium signal.*
- **Enamel pin** — die-struck, hard-fill enamel on metal. · *use when: collectibles, paddock-passes, giveaways.*
- **YKK metal zipper** — durable metal-tooth zipper. · *use when: outerwear, denim; the industry default.*
- **YKK Vislon zipper** — plastic-molded tooth, large scale. · *use when: technical outerwear, bold modern register.*
- **YKK Excella zipper** — polished metal premium zipper. · *use when: luxury outerwear; reads as a deliberate spec.*
- **Snap fastener** — metal snap (ring + post). · *use when: western shirts, work jackets; pearl snaps are western-coded.*
- **Eyelet / grommet** — reinforced hole for laces, drawstrings, or breathability. · *use when: cap rear closure, hoodie ties, work jackets.*
- **Rivet** — metal reinforcement at pocket corners. · *use when: jeans, work pants; copper rivets are heritage-coded.*
- **D-ring / O-ring** — webbing or pull hardware. · *use when: techwear, military-coded outerwear, modern caps.*

### Trim
- **Hangtag** — branded card attached at the neck or sleeve. · *use when: every piece; the first thing a customer touches.*
- **Herringbone tape** — woven cotton tape for interior seam binding. · *use when: heritage interior finishing; signals "well-made."*

*Reference points: YKK (zippers/snaps — industry standard), Albini Group (woven labels), Buttonology (corozo + horn buttons).*

## 9 · Typography & display vocabulary

For the brief's `## Typography` section and the site's display-family choice.

- **Grotesque** — late-19th-century sans-serif (closed apertures, modest contrast). · *use when: heritage modern, paddock, sport register. Examples: Akzidenz-Grotesk, Söhne, Inter.*
- **Neo-grotesque** — mid-century refined sans (Helvetica family). · *use when: minimalist luxury, restrained brands.*
- **Geometric sans** — circle-based sans (Futura, Avenir). · *use when: modernist, mid-century, prep register.*
- **Humanist sans** — calligraphic-influenced sans (Gill, Frutiger). · *use when: editorial, mid-luxury menswear.*
- **Condensed grotesque** — narrow grotesque variant. · *use when: paddock, racing, sports register; pairs with italic. Examples: Barlow Condensed, Oswald.*
- **Italic condensed display** — italic-slant condensed grotesque. · *use when: racing, motorsport, action register; the "26 jersey number" lockup.*
- **Slab serif** — heavy block serifs. · *use when: western, archive, heritage workwear. Examples: Rockwell, Sentinel.*
- **Wide serif** — broad, low-contrast serif. · *use when: archive heritage, country, ranch register. Examples: Recoleta, Hatch.*
- **High-contrast serif** — Didone / fashion serif. · *use when: luxury, fashion-editorial register. Examples: Editorial New, Canela.*
- **Display serif** — large-only ornamental serif. · *use when: hero titles, magazine-cover registers.*
- **Monospace** — fixed-width type. · *use when: spec labels, data, technical detail; the universal "label" font. Examples: JetBrains Mono, Berkeley Mono, IBM Plex Mono, Söhne Mono.*
- **Typewriter mono** — vintage monospace with character. · *use when: archive, heritage tour merch, country register.*
- **Italic stretched numeric** — italicized condensed numerals at large scale. · *use when: jersey backs, edition marks, year stamps (e.g. "26").*
- **Tracked all-caps** — letter-spaced uppercase. · *use when: kicker text, section labels; standard letter-spacing `0.18em–0.28em`.*

*Reference points: Klim Type Foundry (Söhne / Editorial New), Pangram Pangram (ABC Diatype), Google Fonts (Barlow Condensed, JetBrains Mono — OFL-licensed for the OG card builder).*

## 10 · Photography & lighting (for prompt building)

For Round 1 location plates, Round 5 lookbook, Round 6 hero — the prompt's lighting and lens clauses.

- **Golden hour** — the hour after sunrise / before sunset; long warm shadows. · *use when: outdoor lifestyle, hero shots, "aspirational" register.*
- **Blue hour** — twilight, deep blue ambient. · *use when: techwear, moody, after-show register.*
- **First light** — early morning, cool soft tones. · *use when: workwear, "first day on the job" register, athletic.*
- **Overcast / soft box** — diffused even light, low contrast. · *use when: studio mockups, catalog product shots.*
- **Raked light** — light from a steep side angle, exaggerating texture. · *use when: embellishment macros, fabric detail shots.*
- **Top-down / overhead** — light from directly above. · *use when: flat-lay product, line sheet, range grid.*
- **Direct sun + flare** — uncontrolled sun with lens flare. · *use when: surf, summer, "shot on a phone" register; specify the flare corner.*
- **35mm film stock** — fine grain, natural color. · *use when: lookbook, contemporary editorial register.*
- **Medium-format feel** — compressed perspective, smooth tonal range. · *use when: premium catalog, luxury lookbook register.*
- **50mm equivalent** — natural human-eye perspective. · *use when: full-body model shots, no exaggeration.*
- **35mm equivalent** — slightly wide, environmental context. · *use when: hero shots, location-driven editorial.*
- **80mm equivalent / short tele** — compressed background, flattering portraits. · *use when: clean three-quarter portraits, controlled flat-lay.*
- **Shallow depth of field** — narrow focus plane, blurred background. · *use when: embellishment macros, hero subjects.*
- **Slight grain** — film-emulating noise. · *use when: any "not-AI" override; explicit specification helps push outputs away from default AI gloss.*

*Reference points: Cabin Magazine, Garden & Gun (photography registers); Tyler Mitchell, Joshua Gordon (modern fashion photographers worth describing).*

## How to use this glossary

When writing `BRIEF.md`:
- `## Garment language` — pull from sections 1, 2, 3, 4.
- `## Embellishment plan` — pull from sections 5, 6.
- `## Palette` notes on finish — pull from section 7.
- `## Trim & hardware` (if you add one) — pull from section 8.
- `## Typography` — pull from section 9.

When writing **generation prompts** (Phase 3):
- Round 2 mnemonics — pull from sections 5, 6 (embellishment technique) + 9 (wordmark treatment).
- Round 3 mockups — pull from sections 1, 2, 3 (fabric + fit) + 4 (construction) + 5, 6 (embellishment).
- Round 5 / 6 — pull from section 10 (lighting + lens).

**Don't keyword-stuff.** Pick the 2–4 terms per prompt that actually communicate the spec. A prompt with five embroidery techniques will produce a confused composite; a prompt with one specific technique plus one fabric and one finish produces a coherent image.
